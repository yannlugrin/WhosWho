## Architecture

Layers, each only calling the ones below it:

1. **Store**: AceDB setup and defaults; `ns.data` is the account-wide data, `ns.settings` the settings (AceDB profile).
2. **Model**: Identity (self), People (other players), AutomaticChanges (what People changed on its own; People calls it, and it reads people through People). Each owns its part of `ns.data`. No frames or events; the few game functions they call (`time`, the key's entropy sources) are stubbed by the offline tests.
3. **Resolver**: answers "who is this character?" from the model, following the precedence rules.
4. **Trust**: decides whether an incoming record or link is accepted (sender, rank, signature). Built: the signature verification queue (`RecordVerification`).
5. **Comm**: codec, scopes, protocol. AceComm (with ChatThrottleLib) is the single outgoing queue.
6. **UI**: windows, decorations, settings. Reads through the Resolver, writes through the model.

Every file casts the add-on table with `---@cast ns WhosWho.Namespace`. That class, declared in `Core.lua`, lists each field a file puts in `ns`; each module table has a class named after the module (`WhosWho.People`, …), so the language server checks calls across files. A new module adds its field to `WhosWho.Namespace`.

### Event Loop

`Core.lua` registers the events below on its own frame; AceComm handles `CHAT_MSG_ADDON` for the `WhosWho` prefix. Registering an event the client does not know throws on Forever, so any event that may be missing from a build must be registered inside `pcall`.

Implemented:

- `ADDON_LOADED` (own name): AceDB.
- `PLAYER_LOGIN`: keys created if missing; own character entry (name, ruleset, class ID, level, last seen); `Protocol.Start` (comm prefix, announced revision and Guild scope, revision and scope listeners, whether I am in a group and a guild, my friends); roster request (`C_GuildInfo.GuildRoster`); first-login prompt (`IdentityDialogs.AskToLink`, also opened by the settings button) when the character is not registered in the identity, linked or not (`Identity.IsRegistered`): `NewIdentity` while no character is linked, `LinkCharacter` otherwise. **Link** links the character (with `NewIdentity`, the nickname is set first; a refused one is printed and the dialog stays open), **Not this character** registers it unlinked (`linked = false`), closing leaves it unregistered (`linked = nil`); minimap button (`Launcher.Register`); tooltip decoration (`Tooltip.Register`), registered one frame later so it runs after the tooltip post-calls other add-ons register at login.
- `PLAYER_ENTERING_WORLD`: on a login (`isInitialLogin`), not on a `/reload` or a loading screen, the start of my current character's session (`Identity.LoggedIn`), which the announcements to the people I whispered this session use (see Announcements), and the end of a guild joined before (see Workarounds); a guild my current character was removed from while offline, or that disbanded, is handled as a guild left (`Scopes.GuildChanged`); then, on a login or a `/reload` (`isReloadingUi`), `Protocol.AnnounceLogin`.
- `PLAYER_LOGOUT` (also on `/reload`): level and last seen time of the current character, saved right after; the account's last logout (`Identity.LoggedOut`: the character and the time, see Announcements); `Protocol.SessionEnding`, which makes the next login announce when a `REC` is still owed (see Announcements); whispers older than the whisper window removed (`Scopes.ForgetOldWhispers`, see Scopes).
- `PLAYER_CAMPING`, `PLAYER_QUITING` (the 20-second logout or quit countdown starts): logout announcement.
- `Logout` and `Quit`, hooked (`hooksecurefunc`): resting and out of combat, they are immediate, without a countdown: logout announcement.
- The countdown dialogs, hooked: `StaticPopupDialogs.CAMP.OnCancel` for any reason but `"timeout"`, and `StaticPopupDialogs.QUIT.OnHide` with time left, both of which call `CancelLogout`: `Protocol.LogoutCanceled`, which sends nothing, so the next logout is announced again. `LOGOUT_CANCEL` does not fire on Forever.
- `PLAYER_LEVEL_UP`: the same refresh, with the new level from the event (`UnitLevel` still returns the old one); announcement.
- `GUILD_ROSTER_UPDATE`: my current character's guild in `Identity`, `guild` of the characters I hold (see Scopes), and their activity (see Data Model); sends nothing.
- `FRIENDLIST_UPDATE`: `friendOf` of the characters I hold, for my current character (see Scopes), and the activity of the ones online; then `Protocol.FriendListChanged`, the announcement to a friend added (see Announcements).
- `BN_FRIEND_LIST_SIZE_CHANGED`, `BN_FRIEND_INFO_CHANGED`: `bnetFriend` of the characters I hold (`Scopes.BnetFriendListChanged`). Registered only when the client knows them (`C_EventUtils.IsEventValid`).
- `PLAYER_GUILD_UPDATE` (player): a guild left clears my current character's guild, and, once none of my characters is in it, what I hold through it goes (`People.GuildLeft`, the opt-in, my list's revision); a guild joined requests the roster; then `Protocol.GuildChanged`, the announcement on joining a guild (see Announcements).
- `CHAT_MSG_WHISPER`: the sender's name and GUID, saved for the senders' GUIDs only (see Scopes); the sender's activity.
- `CHAT_MSG_WHISPER_INFORM`: the receiver's name and GUID, saved for the senders' GUIDs and the Whispers scope (see Scopes), and its activity; then `Protocol.Whispered`, the announcement to that player (see Announcements).
- `GROUP_ROSTER_UPDATE`: the activity of the members connected (`Scopes.GroupRosterChanged`); `Protocol.GroupRosterChanged`, the announcement on joining a group (see Announcements).

Planned:

- `PLAYER_ENTERING_WORLD`: pruning, throttled.
- `BN_CHAT_MSG_ADDON`: protocol over Battle.net.

### Secret Values

Forever runs the Midnight-era API: some chat, unit and roster values may be secret. Every value read from the game goes through `issecretvalue` (shimmed to `false` where absent) before any comparison, concatenation or table key. A secret value is only ever displayed, never stored or sent. Outside instances no secret value was seen (chat, roster, friends, units, tooltips, menus); inside a dungeon neither, in and out of combat (group members' names and GUIDs, checked in game); during a boss fight is *to verify in game*.

## Data Model

Characters are keyed by **GUID** (`Player-<id>-<hex>`). Forever has no realms: a character has a whole name "First Surname" (`UnitFullName` returns both parts for any unit, checked in game; `UnitNameUnmodified` only for the player), unique per **ruleset** (Normal, PvP, RP, Hardcore, from `C_GameRules`) within a region. Each character caches its whole name, ruleset and class, the last two as numbers: the ruleset as `Record.RULESET` (1 Normal, 2 PvP, 3 RP, 4 Hardcore), the class as the game's class ID (`UnitClass`, third return). Whole name + ruleset is the secondary key, used to resolve a name seen in a menu or typed by the player to a GUID. AceDB's own character and realm keys already follow this (`RegionalUniqueNamesEnabled()`).

Everything is an **identity** (a person): a nickname and characters.

| Kind | Who writes it | Authority |
|---|---|---|
| **Own identity** | This account | Authoritative about itself |
| **Shared identity** | Another player, about themselves (signed record) | Authoritative: its characters and main |
| **Manual identity** | This player, for someone without the add-on (ID `M<n>`) | Local claim |
| **Added alt** | This player, on a shared or manual identity | Local claim, state `added` |
| **Guild identity** | The guild master or an officer, for someone in the guild without an identity (ID `G<hex>`, see Guild Sharing, Guild records) | Guild claim, shared with that guild only |
| **Guild link** | The guild master or an officer: a character in the guild on a guild identity or a shared one | Guild claim, state `guild`; a player's record wins |

Each character of a person has a **state**:

| State | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `confirmed` | Listed in the player's record, and a message sent from that character carried the identity |
| `listed` | Listed in the player's record, not confirmed yet: trusted by default, shown as unconfirmed |
| `relayed` | Listed in the player's record, which a trusted member of my guild passed on, not the player (see Guild Lists); not confirmed yet |
| `added` | Added by me (manual identity, or an alt on a shared one) |
| `guild` | Linked by my guild: its guild master or an officer, not the player, on a guild identity or a shared one (see Guild Sharing, Guild records) |

Each person has **one table of characters** (`chars`), each with its state; that table is what the frames list. A shared person also keeps the player's **record** exactly as received: what the player declared, signed, used to compare revisions and to relay it to others. A new revision copies the player's nickname, main and characters into the person, keeping confirmed states, activity and my added characters; everything else reads the person, not the record.

Each character also carries its **activity**: `level` and `lastSeen`. It is not part of the record. `People.Confirm` records it from the character's own message (once the character is confirmed in that identity, that is all a confirmation changes); `People.Activity` takes it from what the game shows, for any character I hold, shared or mine (several at once, one change): a guild member online, a connected group member, an online friend, a whisper sent or received is seen now (with its level when the source gives it); a guild member offline in the roster gets its level only (an activity without `lastSeen`), never a last seen time: I didn't see it. It keeps a last seen time only when more recent and a level only when higher, and never confirms. A record stored for an identity whose characters my roster lists gives them their level from the roster as well, not a last seen time (`Scopes.KeepGuildMembers`). `People.Create` and `People.AddCharacter` take the level when I can see the character I add. `People.Find(guid)` returns the person and the character's whole entry, `People.Exists(guid)` only whether a person holds it. My own characters keep their level and last seen time in `Identity`, refreshed at login, logout and level-up.

Each person also keeps **`updatedAt`**, when it last changed: created, a record of the player's accepted, an announcement from one of its characters, a character declaring no identity, or one of my changes (nickname, note, characters, main). With `lastSeen`, it dates every person, even one with no character ever seen, for a later purge (see Open Questions).

A character belongs to one person: one that already belongs to a person cannot be added to another. When a player's record lists a character of one of my manual identities, the manual identity merges into the player's: the characters the record lists become listed, the others stay added, my nickname for it moves unless I already named the player's identity, my note about it moves too (added below the note I already had on the player's identity), and the manual identity is removed. An alt I added to another shared identity moves alone. A manual identity's main cannot be removed (`RemoveCharacter` returns `"main"`): change the main first, or forget the person. A character another player's identity holds stays there: a later record listing it doesn't get it, until a message sent from that character confirms the later identity, which then takes it (a character transferred to another account, or a false claim). If another identity holds a shared person's main, the person is still shown under that main's name from its record. A confirmation for a character the stored record doesn't list yet (a new alt announcing itself before its revision arrives) waits in memory, for the session only, and applies when the revision listing it is accepted; nothing unproven is saved. `People` keeps one GUID → person index, updated on each change. **Forget everyone else** (`People.ForgetAll`) removes every person; the `forgotten` list and the automatic changes stay, and a person who announces again is asked for their record again. It also forgets my guild lists' revisions (`GuildList.Forget`), so my next list of each guild is a first one, older than everyone's, and the guild's identities come back from another member's list (see Guild Lists).

A character that declared no identity (`NOID`, see Removals) is kept in `noIdentity`, by GUID, with the time of the last `NOID` received from it (for a cleanup, later): a record that lists it, an older one relayed later for example, does not store it, until a message from that character confirms an identity again; but my guild vouches for its members: a record my guild sent (on `GUILD`, or a guild member's whisper, relayed or not) stores the characters my roster lists (`Scopes.GuildMembersIn`), listed, even after their `NOID`; their mark stays until they confirm an identity. A newer revision without characters, or one whose characters all declared no identity, means the player unlinked them all: the person is forgotten, with my nickname and the alts I added to it. Its ID and that revision stay in `forgotten`, so an older copy relayed later is stale; a newer revision with characters brings the player back.

Every change to my links that I did not make is kept in `AutomaticChanges` for the Review tab. `People` adds one for each kind: `merged` (my manual identity merged into a player's), `moved` (an alt I added to a shared identity moved to another player's), `taken` (a character a player listed, taken by the identity a message from it confirmed, possibly that player's main), all three from the same place whether a record or a confirmation caused them. A character the player removed from their identity (a new revision no longer lists it, or a `NOID` from it, see Removals) leaves silently, and so does an identity whose player unlinked every character: showing them would invite me to add them again by hand. Each change keeps its time, kind, `from` (the identity the characters left) and `to` (the one they joined: merged, moved and taken only), each as `{ id, nickname, customNickname, main }`: `from` as it was before the change, `to` as it is when the characters join it (with the revision that caused the change already applied), the characters with their state then, and a read flag. Only the 100 most recent are kept. People calls `AutomaticChanges.Record` just before each change, which takes the snapshots. `AutomaticChanges.List()` gives them most recent first, `UnreadCount()` the number on the tab, `MarkRead(change)` marks one read. For merged, moved and taken, a change also keeps `toChars`, the characters the identity they joined already had, and each changed character's `stateAfter`, its state there once the change is complete (listed or confirmed: the player declares it; added: still mine). These changes happen inside an operation: `People.Accept` (storing a revision's characters) and `People.Confirm` call `AutomaticChanges.BeginOperation(id)`, which keeps the identity's characters as they are, and `EndOperation()`, which completes each change recorded during the operation with its `toChars` and `stateAfter`, then saves it; a merge's characters only get their final state once the whole revision is applied. A saved change is complete and never changed again, apart from its read flag.

A shared identity counts as **confirmed** once one of its characters is; until then (for example a record relayed by someone else) it is shared but not confirmed.

Any identity can carry **my nickname** (`People.Rename`), shared ones included. It replaces, for me only, the nickname that applies without it.

### Records

```
Identity record (shared)
  v         format version (Record.VERSION, 2)
  id        identity ID: the public key, hex
  rev       revision, +1 on every change, at most 2^31 - 1
  nickname  optional override; the main character's name is shown otherwise
  main      GUID of the main character, one of chars; absent when chars is empty
  chars     { [guid] = { name, ruleset, classID } }   characters the owner declares;
            ruleset one of Record.RULESET, classID a class the game knows; guild = { forced, main },
            only in the record sent to that guild's members (see Guild Sharing, Enforced linking)
  guild     { consent }   consent: the record may be passed on to the members of the guilds of the owner's
            characters; it is the owner's Guild scope, so changing that scope makes a new revision
  sig       signature over every other field (see Signed bytes)
```

Every signed record (identity records, guild settings and guild records) is signed the same way, so a field a later version adds is verified, kept and passed on whole by the versions before it, which show only the fields they know:

- **Signed bytes** (`Record.SignedBytes(prefix, record)`): a prefix naming the kind of record (`WWI` identity, `WWG` guild settings, `WWGR` guild record), then every field but `sig`, whatever its name: each value with its type (`s<length>:<bytes>`, `i<integer>;`, `t` or `f`, and `{…}` for a table, its entries sorted by their key's bytes); strings, integers up to 2^31 - 1, booleans and tables only, keyed by strings or integers. `Record.CanonicalWithoutRevision` leaves `rev` out too, to compare what two revisions say.
- **Limits**, checked while the signed bytes are built, by each format's `Validate`, before the record enters the verification queue: 1 024 keys in all, a depth of 6 tables, 4 KB per string, and 32 KB of signed bytes (`Record.MAX_SIGNED_LENGTH`: hashing them takes about 150 ms in game, in one frame; measured: 4.6 ms per KB). The payload of a `REC` or a `GSET` follows this limit with a margin (see Protocol), so a record they allow is never refused on the way. Each limit is at least twice what the largest legitimate record needs, built by a test from the field limits (`Record.MAX_CHARACTERS`, `Record.MAX_NAME_LENGTH` in two-byte letters, guild markers): today 6 930 bytes. A limit raised later is refused by every older client.
- **Known fields** are checked as strictly as before; unknown ones only against the limits. `v` is raised only for a field the versions before it must not ignore: they refuse a record of another version.
- **Version 1**, up to 0.2.0, was signed over a fixed list of fields, so a record holding any other field was refused. It is refused now. What I hold from it stays shown until a newer revision arrives. My own record gets a new revision once (migration step 4, see Saved Variables), so the others fetch it: one they hold at the same revision would not be asked for again. Guild settings held in version 1 stay in use by the members who hold them; a member who never held them refuses them, until the guild master or an officer changes the settings once.

```
Guild record (GuildRecord; see Guild Sharing, Guild records)
  v           format version; a higher one is refused: raised only for a field older clients must not ignore
  guild       club ID
  id          the identity it is about: a player's identity ID, or a guild identity's "G<16 hex>"
  nickname    guild identity only, optional
  main        guild identity only: GUID, one of chars
  chars       { [guid] = { name, ruleset, classID } }   characters the guild links to that identity
  signer      identity ID of the member of the trusted rank who signed it
  signerGuid  the character it was signed from
  rev         server time when signed
  sig         signature over every other field
  (later: the guild's info about the person, such as a role, a note or a join date, and about each character)
```

### Character Confirmations

An identity record is the owner's claim about which characters are theirs. Add-on users are trusted by default: listed characters are shown right away, as unconfirmed. A character is confirmed once a message **sent from that character** carried that identity (the server authenticates the sender): an announcement (see Protocol). A character confirmed by another identity is shown under that identity, not the claimant's. False claims are not fully handled (see Open Questions).

### Resolution

For a character GUID:

1. This account's own linked character.
2. Otherwise the one person holding it: a shared identity (the first to list it, or the one a message from that character confirmed), or my own link (an alt added to a shared identity, or a manual identity).

The nickname shown, for me: my nickname, otherwise the identity's own (the player's for a shared identity, the guild's for a guild identity), otherwise the main character's name. `People.IdentityNickname` gives the identity's own nickname whatever overrides it (the player's, otherwise the main character's name). `Identity.Nickname` applies the same rule to my own identity: my nickname, otherwise my main's name. `Resolver.Resolve` returns the identity ID, that nickname, `mine` (this account's own identity) and what the glyphs need: `shared` (the player shares this identity) and `state` (`confirmed`, `listed` or `added`).

Nicknames are shown by the receiving add-on only, in the lines, tooltips and lists it decorates; outgoing chat is never modified.

In chat, `ChatFrameUtil.AddSenderNameFilter` gets each line's decorated sender name with the event's arguments; the filter adds the nickname, in light grey and round brackets, after the name, and the game puts the result inside the player link: `[Ann Main (Annie)]: hello`. It adds nothing when the `chat.nickname.enable` setting is off, when the sender's name or GUID is secret (the game already skips the filter when the decorated name is), for my own characters, for a character no identity holds, or when the nickname is the character's name.

## Protocol

Measured on build 1.60.1. Forever has no group finder, so every group is a home group: `PARTY` or `RAID`, never `INSTANCE_CHAT`. Inside a dungeon, in and out of combat, add-on messages pass on `PARTY` and `C_ChatInfo.InChatMessagingLockdown()` is false (checked in game); during a boss fight is *to verify in game*.

- A message over 255 characters returns `Success` but is silently dropped: never rely on the send result for size.
- Byte 0 truncates a message; bytes 1–255 arrive intact.
- A `WHISPER` to an offline character returns `Success` and prints nothing on either side; the message is lost (checked in game).
- The sender argument is the whole Forever name, `First Surname` with a space and no realm, while `UnitName` returns only the first name. A name alone never confirms a character: the GUID comes from the game's own sources (see Senders).
- Whispers never cross rulesets.
- Own `GUILD`, `PARTY` and self-`WHISPER` messages come back to the sender, so handlers must recognise and skip their own messages by content.

- One prefix, `WhosWho`. AceComm handles splitting messages over 255 characters; ChatThrottleLib is the single throttled outgoing queue. Each client may send about 10 messages per prefix, then about 1 per second (whispers outside instances are exempt); sending too much data at once, even over several prefixes, can disconnect the client.
- Battle.net is not built yet: AceComm cannot send over `BNSendGameData`, and no Battle.net friend has been seen in Forever yet. Every Battle.net path below (friends audience, sender lookup, `GET` and `REC`) waits for an in-game round trip.
- Payloads: LibSerialize then LibDeflate, encoded for the add-on channel. Announcements and `GET`s stay short plain text so they fit in one message.
- Unpacking a payload costs about 41 ms per KB received at worst in game (LibDeflate unpacks without limit: 2.6 KB became 1 MB in 106 ms, 10.4 KB 4 MB in 423 ms). So `Protocol` reads the type first (`Codec.Type`) and unpacks only a `REC`, `GSET` or `GLIST` from a sender it takes that type from (`PAYLOAD_SENDERS`: a `REC` from a channel I share on, settings from a member of my guild, a list from a member while I exchange lists); the diagnostic mode prints the others. `Codec` then refuses, before unpacking, a payload longer than its limit: 34 KB for a `REC` or a `GSET`: the limit of their signed bytes (see Records) and 6% more, since a payload may be slightly larger than its signed bytes (0.4% for incompressible content); 128 KB for a `GLIST`. The largest legitimate ones, measured with the libraries: a `REC` of 50 characters with long multi-byte names and guild markers, 2 470 bytes; a `GSET`, 232; a `GLIST` of 2 000 identities, 90 731. A limit raised later is refused by every older client. AceComm still puts together a message of any length before it reaches `Protocol`.
- Every message starts with a protocol version. Unknown versions and types are ignored, never errors.
- **Gathering**: everything that goes out after a wait goes through one mechanism (`newGathering` in `Protocol`), so that several calls close together send once: each call pushes the send back to its wait from now, never brings it forward, and the send happens at the latest at a cap after the first call, so nothing waits forever. A call during the send starts the next batch. The waits, with their cap: answering `GET`s of identities I relay, `GIGET`s and `GLGET`s (`REQUEST_WAIT`), answering announcements and comparing the guild lists they carry (`ANNOUNCEMENT_WAIT`), my changes to my guild's settings and records (`GUILD_CHANGES_WAIT`): 5 seconds, 30 at most; my change of revision and my `REC`s (`RECORD_WAIT`): 15 seconds for a change of revision (`revisionWaitSeconds`), 5 for a `GET`, 300 at most, and held, checked again every 5 seconds, while sending is locked or once my logout announcement went out.

| Type | Content | Sent |
|---|---|---|
| `ANNOUNCE` | identity ID, revision, the sending character's level, whether the sender answers a `GET` now, whether the sender wants the receiver's announcement, the revision of the guild list the sender holds, the revision of the guild settings the sender holds | At login, level-up and logout, and after a change of revision, from a linked character, to the audiences of the enabled scopes; to the players a scope turned on, a group or guild joined or a friend added brings into them; to a player I whisper; in answer to an announcement that wants one (see Announcements). Empty (`1 ANNOUNCE 0 0 0 0 1 0 0`, no identity) from a character sharing nothing with its guild, to that guild (see Linked and Anonymous Characters) |
| `GET` | 1 to 3 identity IDs (0.1.0 names one) | To the sender of an announcement with a newer revision that accepts a `GET`, by whisper (`BNSendGameData` to a Battle.net friend); to members holding a guild list, for the identities it has newer (see Guild Lists) |

The 30 and 60 second values below are starting values, to adjust in game.

### Channels

A message for several players goes out on the cheapest channel that reaches them, in this order: `GUILD`, then `PARTY` or `RAID`, then one message each (`WHISPER`, or `BNSendGameData` to a Battle.net friend). A member channel (`GUILD`, `PARTY`, `RAID`: one message reaches every member) is used only when its scope is enabled, since it reaches every member of that audience: `GUILD` with the Guild scope, `PARTY` or `RAID` with the Group scope. A player reached by a member channel gets no individual copy: a friend who is also a guild member counts as a guild member. Until its next login, a character that joined its guild sends nothing on `GUILD`: one message to each online member takes its place (see Workarounds).

### Linked and Anonymous Characters

The character I play is **linked** (in my identity) or **anonymous** (never linked, or unlinked). An anonymous character never sends a message that carries or asks for an identity: no announcement, no `GET`, no `REC`, no answer. A character I unlinked sends one message only, `NOID`, which carries nothing (see Removals). A character whose announcement doesn't reach its guild (anonymous, the Guild scope off, or a guild enforcing linking not answered or refused: `Scopes.AnnouncesToGuild`) sends that guild an **empty announcement** at login and on joining it (`Codec.EmptyAnnouncement`: no identity, no revision, no level, `wantsAnnouncement` = `1`): the members answer with theirs, which carry the guild's settings revision, and nobody can ask it for a record. 0.1.x refuses it (no identity ID). Receiving one only queues my answer (see Announcements); my own, back on `GUILD`, is recognised by my name.

What an anonymous character does with what arrives depends on my account:

- **Another of my characters is linked**: it updates what I already hold, from a channel my scopes accept (see Receiving a Record): a newer revision of an identity I hold (a `REC` that passes, for example someone else's `GET` answered on `GUILD`), confirmations, level and last seen time. It stores a new identity only when one of my linked characters shares with that player: a `REC` on `GUILD`, while a linked character of mine is in that guild and ruleset (`Scopes.LinkedCharacterInGuild`). From the group, a friend or a whisper, never: there, only this anonymous character knows the player (a friend of one of my linked characters can't be recognised in an identity I don't hold yet). It never asks for anything.
- **No character is linked** (never linked, or after Forget me): everything that arrives is ignored, `NOID` included, but announcements and my guild's settings (`GSGET`, `GSET`), which hold no identity, and what a guild I opted in to sends (see Enforced linking); an announcement that wants mine is still answered, with `NOID` from a character I unlinked.

**Linking the character I play**: when it was anonymous at login, the announcement of the change of revision that links it has `wantsAnnouncement` = `1` on every channel, like a login announcement: while anonymous, it asked nobody, so the players online send theirs back.

### Announcements

`1 ANNOUNCE <id> <rev> <level> <acceptsGet> <wantsAnnouncement> <guildListRev> <guildSettingsRev>`, each flag being `1` or `0`; about 105 bytes, one message. `guildSettingsRev` is the revision of the settings record the sender holds for its current guild, `0` without one; 0.1.0 sends no such field, read as `0`. `guildListRev` is the revision of the guild list the sender holds for its current guild, `<server time>.<hash>`, or `0` while it exchanges none (see Guild Lists). Fields after these are ignored, so a later version can add some without breaking this one.

Confirming characters is the core of the system, so one message carries the confirmation, the activity and the revision, and every announcement carries the identity.

- **Sent** at login, at level-up and at logout, and after a change of revision, once its wait has ended (see Sending a Record), from a linked character, to the audiences of the enabled scopes (see Channels), and, except at login, to the people I whispered this session (see below). A change of revision sends no `REC` by itself: the players holding an older revision ask for it with a `GET`, and the confirmation waits for the record on their side. When the Guild scope was turned on since the last change of revision announced, the copy on `GUILD` has `wantsAnnouncement` = `1`: until then the guild members ignored my messages and I theirs, so they send theirs back. At logout it goes out directly through `C_ChatInfo.SendAddonMessage` (the throttled queue would not empty in time), with `acceptsGet` = `0`: the sender is leaving, so a `GET` would get no answer. It goes out once per logout, as soon as the logout is certain: when the logout or quit countdown starts, or on an immediate logout or quit. During a real logout's `PLAYER_LOGOUT`, `SendAddonMessage` fails (`GeneralError`) and the guild and friend list are already cleared. Once it went out, nothing else does (no announcement, answer, `GET` or `REC`), so it is the last message the other players get from me; the `REC` wait holds, as with the editing lock, and a change or a `REC` it was waiting for makes the next login announce (see Quick relog). A canceled countdown sends nothing and lifts that: the wait goes on, and the players who dropped a `GET` because of the logout announcement ask at my next announcement. A `/reload` sends no logout announcement, and its login announcement is skipped (see Quick relog): the players online see no change.
- **Quick relog**: the login announcement is not sent when the character logging in is the one of the account's last logout (`lastLogout`, saved at `PLAYER_LOGOUT`, so a `/reload` counts), less than 5 minutes ago, and it would announce the revision my last announcement to every audience with `acceptsGet` = `1` already carried (`lastBroadcastRevision`, saved by the login, level-up and change of revision announcements). The players online heard that revision moments ago. At `PLAYER_LOGOUT`, when a `REC` is still owed (requesters waiting, the wait running, or a `REC` the queue has not sent yet), `lastBroadcastRevision` is forgotten, so the next login announces anyway: the requesters dropped their `GET` at my logout announcement (after a `/reload`, without one, they send it again 60 seconds later). Another character logging in announces, since that announcement confirms it, and so does a revision not announced yet: a change whose 15-second wait had not ended at the logout, whose logout announcement carried it with `acceptsGet` = `0`, or a renamed character found at login. The sender may set `0` for other reasons later (a raid or a battleground) without any change to receivers.
- **Reaching players who come later**: a broadcast only reaches the players online and in my audiences when it leaves. The others get my announcement in these cases, from a linked character, each to a player `Scopes.Allows`:
  - **Joining a group** (`Protocol.GroupRosterChanged`, from not in a group to in a group; in a group at login, the login announcement already reaches it), with the Group scope, or **turning the Group scope on** while in a group: one announcement on `PARTY` or `RAID`, sent right away.
  - **Joining a guild** (`Protocol.GuildChanged`, from not in a guild to in a guild; in a guild at login, the login announcement already reaches it), with the Guild scope: one announcement to each online member, by whisper, once the guild's roster arrives with its club ID (`Protocol.GuildRosterChanged`; see Workarounds). Turning the Guild scope on makes a change of revision, whose announcement brings the guild's back (see above).
  - **Adding a friend** (`Protocol.FriendListChanged`: a GUID that was not in my friend list at the previous `FRIENDLIST_UPDATE`), with the Friends scope, or **turning the Friends scope on**: one announcement to each of those friends online that `GUILD` and the group channel do not reach, sent right away. A friend logging in is not a friend added: their own login announcement reaches me when they list me too, and a friend who doesn't list me would accept nothing from me anyway. Turning the Whispers scope on sends nothing: it applies to the players I whisper from then on.
  - **Whispering a player** (`Protocol.Whispered`), with the Whispers scope, when neither `GUILD`, the group channel nor the Friends scope reaches them (they get my other announcements): one announcement to that player, sent right away, when none reached them this session, when the last one carried an older revision, or when it left more than 30 minutes ago (it refreshes my level and last seen time on their side). A whisper is the only time I know the player is online, so at login only the people whispered just before the previous logout are in my audience; the other broadcasts reach the people I whispered this session (below). The player answers it like a login if their scopes allow me, which for the Whispers scope means they whispered me within their whisper window. A whisper received sends nothing: I share with the players I whisper, so a player gets my announcement once I answer them.
  - **The people I whispered this session**: the level-up, change of revision and logout announcements also go to each shared person the character I play whispered since its login (`loggedInAt`, set at `PLAYER_ENTERING_WORLD` on a login, so a `/reload` keeps it) within the whisper window, with the Whispers scope: one message to the **confirmed** character I last whispered with, sent or received (the one they most likely play; a whisper they sent only picks the character, never adds a person), unless `GUILD`, the group channel or the Friends scope reaches it. They come after the guild, the group and the friends: at logout, the messages past the client's limit are dropped, the last ones first. At login, only the people the same character whispered in the 30 minutes before its previous logout, when the account's last logout was that character's (`lastLogout`) and the whisper is still within the whisper window: a short absence, mid-conversation; the others may not be online.
  - **Answering an announcement** that wants one (`wantsAnnouncement` = `1`): mine goes back to its sender, every time. A player logging in may have missed everything I sent before, even a broadcast that left just as they logged in or a message that arrived just after they logged out, so a login is always answered; a group join is answered the same way, being rare.

  `wantsAnnouncement` is `1` on the login, group join, guild join, friend added, scope turned on and whisper announcements only, and on the `GUILD` copy of a change of revision after the Guild scope was turned on. Every other announcement, answers included, has `0`, so an answer never brings another one. The receiver answers only a sender `Scopes.Allows`, so a player I whisper who never whispered me sends me nothing. `wantsAnnouncement` = `1` always comes with `acceptsGet` = `1`.

  | Sent at | `acceptsGet` | `wantsAnnouncement` |
  |---|---|---|
  | Login (none on a quick relog) | `1` | `1` |
  | Joining a group or a guild, a friend added, the Group or Friends scope turned on | `1` | `1` |
  | Level-up | `1` | `0` |
  | Answer to an announcement | `1` | `0` |
  | Whispering a player | `1` | `1` |
  | Change of revision | `1` | `0` (`1` on `GUILD` after the Guild scope was turned on) |
  | Logout | `0` | `0` |

  The answers wait together, like the requesters of a `REC` (see Sending a Record): a player goes in a queue of pending names, once, and one gathering (`ANNOUNCEMENT_WAIT`, see Protocol) sends to them all, in the fewest messages: one on `GUILD` reaches every pending name in my current guild, one on `PARTY` or `RAID` every pending name in my group, each other name gets its own whisper, with the same order at equal count. A pending name `Scopes.Allows` no longer is dropped; an announcement with `acceptsGet` = `0` from a pending name (the player left) removes it. Each player an answer, a whisper announcement or an announcement to a friend added or to the friends of a scope turned on reached is kept with the revision it carried and when it left, in memory for the session (`announcements`), for the whisper rule above; broadcasts do not count, since a player may log in just as one leaves.
- **Removals**: a character unlinked from my identity sends `1 NOID` in place of each of its announcements, and nothing else: at its logins, level-ups and logouts, in place of the announcement of the change of revision that unlinks it when I play it, in answers, and wherever an announcement goes to players who may not know it yet (a group or guild joined, a friend added, a scope turned on, a player I whisper), with the same rules and limits. Indefinitely, until it is linked again; a player met only in a group or by whisper learns the removal that way. It never sends my identity ID again. The server authenticates the sender, so only that character can say it; repeated, it is a no-op. A player who holds it in a shared identity, listed or confirmed, removes it, silently (see Data Model); a character they added themselves stays, being their own claim. They also learn the removal from my next revision announced by a linked character (`GET`, then a `REC` that no longer lists it), which is how a character I unlink from the window while playing another one leaves. Unlinking every character is the same case: each removed character's `NOID` removes it, and an identity left without characters is forgotten. **Forget me** (`Identity.Forget`) unlinks every character and removes the nickname in one revision; every character becomes unregistered (`linked = nil`), so each one is asked again at its next login, and each one I played while linked sends `NOID`. Without a server, a player never online at the same time as one of my removed characters keeps it listed.
- **`NOID` received**: when the sender's GUID is known (see Senders), the character leaves the shared identity holding it and its GUID goes in `noIdentity` (see Data Model); an identity left without characters is forgotten. My own characters are ignored.
- **Received**: my own identity ID is ignored. When the sender's GUID is known (see Senders), `People.Confirm(id, guid, level)`: the first one confirms the character, the next ones only update its level and last seen time. The sender found in my roster or friend list gets its `guild` or `friendOf` (`Scopes.SetRelationships`). When the revision is newer than what `People` holds (`People.IsNewer`: not held, or held at an older revision, and not forgotten at that revision or a later one) and `acceptsGet` is `1`, a `GET` (see Requesting a Record), only from a sender my scopes allow (see Receiving a Record). When `wantsAnnouncement` is `1` and my scopes allow the sender, my answer joins the pending names (see above). An announcement on `GUILD` from a sender my roster does not list (a member who joined since it was read) requests the roster (`C_GuildInfo.GuildRoster`).

### Senders

The sender of an add-on message is its whole name, authenticated by the server; a Battle.net message gives a game account ID instead. A confirmation needs the sender's GUID, taken from what the game itself says about that name, looked up when the announcement arrives, whatever channel it came on (`Scopes.SenderGuid`): what the sender is to me decides, so a guild member's answer by whisper confirms it as well as its message on `GUILD`. The sources, tried in this order:

| Audience | Source of the sender's GUID |
|---|---|
| Guild | the guild roster (`GetGuildRosterInfo`, name and GUID of every member, offline ones included) |
| Group | the group's units: whole name from `UnitFullName`, GUID from `UnitGUID` (checked in game) |
| Friends | `C_FriendList.GetFriendInfo(name).guid` (*to verify in game*) |
| Whispers | the saved whispers within the whisper window, sent or received, of the ruleset I play (other player's name and GUID from the whisper chat events, arguments 2 and 12; see Scopes) |
| Battle.net friends | `C_BattleNet.GetGameAccountInfoByID(id).playerGuid` (*to verify in game*) |

Names match exactly, never by spelling variants; the roster spells them exactly like the add-on sender (checked in game). Without a match there is no confirmation; the record is still fetched.

### Scopes

Guild and Friends are on by default, Whispers and Group off; nothing goes out until a character is linked, and nothing from an anonymous character but `NOID` (see Linked and Anonymous Characters); unlinking every character makes others forget the identity through the removed characters' `NOID` (see Removals).

**Sharing goes both ways**: I only get the identity of a player I share mine with. The same test, `Scopes.Allows`, decides whether I send my record to a player and whether I use a record or ask for one from them (see Announcements and Receiving a Record).

Two separate questions, for each player asking for my record:

1. **Whether my scopes allow sharing with that person**, `Scopes.Allows`. The requesting character is allowed when it is, for my current character, a member of a guild I share with (`Scopes.SharesWithGuild`; `C_GuildInfo.MemberExistsByName`, callable by add-ons, checked in game; or the sender of a message on `GUILD` my roster does not list yet, since the game delivers `GUILD` messages only from members of my guild, until the roster lists it or my guild changes; until the roster loads, at login, a confirmed character of that name whose `guild` is my current character's; the same for `Scopes.GuildReaches`, so the login announcement on `GUILD` is not doubled by a whisper to a guild member I whispered or a friend in the guild), a WoW friend (`C_FriendList.GetFriendInfo`) or Battle.net friend with the Friends scope, a group member with the Group scope, or a player one of my characters of the ruleset I play whispered within the whisper window with the Whispers scope. A whisper received never allows a player: anyone can whisper me, and their record, nickname included, would land in my people list. It is also allowed when it is a confirmed character of a person whose confirmed characters include one with a `guild` among my characters' guilds that I share with (the Guild scope; a guild enforcing linking, only once my identity is complete there: the same rule as for my current guild), a non-empty `friendOf` (Friends scope) or a `whisperedAt` within the whisper window (Whispers scope). Sharing is about people: an alt of a player my scopes allow gets my record, and their account already holds it anyway.
2. **The channel**, from the requesting character and my current character only (see Channels): `GUILD` if it is in my current guild, `PARTY` or `RAID` if it is in my group, otherwise one message to it.

The relationships behind the first question are stored, since a character only sees its own guild roster and friend list. They are what my characters saw themselves, never sent by the other player, so they are kept on any character I hold, and move with it to another identity. Only **confirmed characters** count for sharing, so a record listing a character never makes it count:

- `guild`: the guild in which one of my characters saw this character, written from that character's roster: set for each character in it, cleared for a character with that `guild` no longer in it, also set when an announcement comes from a character found in the roster. It counts only while one of my characters of the same ruleset is in that guild; `Identity` keeps each of my characters' guild. The guild is its club ID (`C_Club.GetGuildClubId()`, a number on Forever although the API documentation says a string; checked in game; that two characters of one guild get the same value is *to verify in game*). Nothing says club IDs are unique across rulesets, so a guild is its club ID and the ruleset of the characters in it.
- `friendOf`: which of my characters has this character as a WoW friend, each of my characters writing only its own entry from its friend list, also set when an announcement comes from a character found in that list. Battle.net friends are account-wide and checked live.
- `bnetFriend`: the BattleTag of my Battle.net friend seen playing this character (`gameAccountInfo.playerGuid`), from the whole friend list each time it changes (`People.UpdateBnetFriends`); cleared once that BattleTag is no longer in the list. An empty list (Battle.net disconnected, or not loaded yet) changes nothing. It is not used for sharing, only by the People tab: its Friends filter, and the BattleTag in the detail panel (*to verify in game*: no Battle.net friend seen in Forever yet).
- `whisperedAt`: when one of my characters last whispered this character (`time()`), set on each whisper I send to a character I hold, and, for a character I did not hold yet, when a stored record brings it (`Scopes.KeepSentWhispers`, from the saved whispers). It counts only within the whisper window, and is cleared once older at logout.

**Clear history**, next to the Whispers scope in the settings, empties the saved whispers and clears every `whisperedAt` (`Scopes.ForgetWhispers`, after a confirmation): the players I whispered are no longer allowed through that scope until I whisper them again.

**Whispers** are saved, account-wide (`whispers`), by the other character's GUID: its name, the ruleset (the same as my character's: whispers never cross rulesets), when one of my characters last whispered it (`sentAt`) and last received a whisper from it (`receivedAt`). A whisper counts for the **whisper window**, a setting (`whisperHours`: 1, 3, 6, 12, 24, 72 or 168 hours, 3 by default; greyed out in the settings while the Whispers scope is off): a whisper sent within it allows the player (Whispers scope), and a whisper sent or received within it gives the sender's GUID for a confirmation. Every use compares with the window, so a whisper stops counting as soon as it is older; at logout (`Scopes.ForgetOldWhispers`), the whispers neither sent nor received within the window are removed, and the `whisperedAt` older than it cleared. A `/reload` or a new login keeps them, so the players I whispered stay allowed for the whole window.

A requesting character found only through a person is recognised by its whole name and my current character's ruleset among confirmed characters (the secondary key, see Data Model): whispers never cross rulesets, so that pair names one character. The risk is limited to sending a `REC`: a confirmed alt deleted or renamed, whose name someone else takes, would get my record. Confirmations never use names alone.

Audiences per scope:

- **Guild**: `GUILD` channel.
- **Friends**: `WHISPER` to online WoW friends; `BNSendGameData` to Battle.net friends in Forever (*to verify in game*). Battle.net IDs are used only locally to address the message and are never part of a payload.
- **Whispers**: one announcement to the player I whisper, and the broadcasts to one character of each person I whispered this session, or, at login, in the 30 minutes before the previous logout (see Announcements). A player is allowed while whispered within the whisper window.
- **Group**: `PARTY` or `RAID` channel, whichever the player is in (checked in game).
- **Selected** (planned, not a priority): `WHISPER` to players I choose one by one; an alternative to Whispers and Group.

Never transmitted: BattleTags, account IDs, Battle.net presence or game account IDs. A Battle.net friend's BattleTag is kept locally on the characters they play (`bnetFriend`).

### Sending a Record

- `Identity` notifies each change of revision. An anonymous character answers no `GET` (see Linked and Anonymous Characters): the requester asks again at my next announcement. A `GET` for my identity puts its sender (name, or Battle.net game account) in a queue of requesters, once per sender, with no lookup yet.
- One gathering covers every pending `REC` (`RECORD_WAIT`, see Protocol): a change of revision pushes it to 15 seconds later, a `GET` to 5 seconds later, and it ends at the latest 300 seconds after it started, so a flood of `GET`s or a long editing session still gets an answer; it is held while sending is locked, or once my logout announcement went out. When it ends:
  - the revision changed since the last one announced this session: an announcement to the audiences of every enabled scope (see Announcements); the players holding an older revision ask for it;
  - then each requester: dropped unless `Scopes.Allows` it. The `REC` reaches the ones kept in the fewest messages: one on `GUILD` reaches every requester in my current guild, one on `PARTY` or `RAID` every requester in my group, and each requester neither reaches gets its own message. A requester reached by a member channel gets no other copy. At equal count: fewer member channels first (a requester alone on its channel gets its own message), then `GUILD` before the group.

    Example: A and B in my guild, B and C in my group, D a friend. A and B are on `GUILD`, C alone on `PARTY`, D on its own message: one `REC` on `GUILD`, one whisper to C, one whisper to D.

    Example: A in my guild, A, B and C in my group, D a friend. A is alone on `GUILD`, A, B and C on `PARTY`, D on its own message: one `REC` on `PARTY`, one whisper to D.

    Example: A and B in my guild, A, B and C in my group. One `REC` on `PARTY` reaches all three: one message, where `GUILD` would need a whisper to C as well.
- `Protocol.LockSending()` and `Protocol.UnlockSending()` take and release the lock; it holds until every holder released it (a count). While it is held, the wait holds, and a scope turned on is not announced yet: once released, the scopes still on are announced (see Announcements), so turning one on and off again sends nothing. The settings hold it while their panel is open (`SettingsPanel` shown), so only what is set when they close goes out; the identity editor is meant to hold it too, so a `REC` goes out once the player is done; a `GET` received meanwhile waits too.
- The announced revision starts, each session, at the current revision: a change found at login (a renamed character) reaches online players through the login announcement and their `GET`s.
- The record is signed once per change of revision (`Identity.SignedRecord`, about 120 ms), whatever the number of changes it holds or of `REC`s sent.
- For about 30 seconds after a `REC` has left (AceComm's send callback, after its last part), `GET`s from the requesters it reached are ignored: a `REC` can wait in the throttled queue, and the `GET`s that crossed it must not send it again.

### Requesting a Record

- A `GET` is remembered with its time; no other `GET` for that identity goes out for about 60 seconds.
- A `REC` with a newer revision clears it, and so does an announcement from that identity with `acceptsGet` = `0` (the owner left). Otherwise, after 60 seconds, the `GET` is sent once more; after that, the next announcement with `acceptsGet` = `1` asks again.

### Receiving a Record

My own identity ID is ignored. An anonymous character stores less (see Linked and Anonymous Characters). A `REC` is used only when it came through a channel I share on: `GUILD` with the Guild scope, `PARTY` or `RAID` with the Group scope, or by whisper or Battle.net from a sender `Scopes.Allows` (see Scopes); otherwise it is ignored, so strangers cannot fill my saved data. It must also bring an identity `People` does not hold yet (a change can reach a friend who never received it) or a newer revision of one it holds, and not an identity forgotten at that revision or a later one. A used `REC` goes to the verification queue (see Signatures), then to `People.Accept`, which also applies the confirmations waiting for it. A `REC` never confirms a character; only announcements do. A `REC` that its player did not send, the sender's name being none of its characters', is a relayed record: see Guild Lists.

### Guild Sharing

Built: the consent, the guild settings, enforced linking with its opt-in, the guild lists and the relayed records, each below. Not built: guild identities and guild links (below), and the parts each item marks.

- **Consent.** A record is passed on to a guild only when its `guild.consent` is true: the owner's Guild scope. A player who shares with friends only is never spread through their guild.
- **Guild settings record.** Built: see Guild Settings. Whether linking characters in the guild is enforced is built (see Enforced linking). Later settings: the rank trusted for relayed identities (see Rank Gate), and which ranks may change the settings.
- **Enforced linking.** Built: a guild setting by which the guild requires its members' characters in it to be known under their identity. It is meant for guilds whose rules link alts to mains: joining the guild accepts it. Leaving the guild is the way out. The player opts in first (below).
  - **Setting**: `enforceLinking` in the guild settings record (see Guild Settings), signed like `enable`, which it needs (`GuildSettings.EnforcesLinking`). The guild panel's Who's Who tab has its checkbox, greyed out while Who's Who isn't enabled for the guild.
  - **Who it applies to**: a member with an identity, for each character of mine in that guild (one this account logged in on, so `Identity` knows it), linked, unlinked or never asked, even with my Guild scope off (`Scopes.EnforcedGuild`: my current guild, when it enforces linking and I have an identity). It goes both ways, for that guild only: as if my Guild scope were on, I also take the guild members' records and answer their `GET`s (`Scopes.SharesWithGuild`, in place of the Guild scope for my current guild). A member without an identity (no add-on, or no character ever linked) sends nothing and is shown as unknown, as today; asking them to link is part of the later step.
  - **What the guild's members get**: the record I send to a member of that guild (my roster lists the requester in it, whatever the channel: guild, group, whisper) also holds each character of mine in that guild that it doesn't, marked `guild = { forced = true }` on the character (see Records; `Identity.UnsignedRecord(clubId, ruleset)`, `Scopes.RecordGuildOf`). With my Guild scope off, it holds my characters in that guild only, every one marked. Everyone else gets my record without them: what a player may know is decided by what I send, and the guild's rule is the only exception. `guild.consent` stays my Guild scope. The marker is a table, so later fields fit (maybe the club ID, public anyway).
  - **Guild main**: the guild sees my main when it is in that guild. Otherwise one of my characters in the guild carries `guild = { main = true }` (with `forced` when the rule added it), and the guild's members show me under its name (my nickname still applies). The record's `main` stays my main when the record holds it, and is absent when it doesn't (Guild scope off, main outside the guild): a record needs `main` among its characters or one character with `guild.main`. The guild main is the one chosen in the guild rule dialog (`Identity.SetGuildMain`, see Opt-in); until one is chosen, the first of my linked characters in the guild by name, otherwise the first of my characters in it by name.
  - **One revision**: what each player gets is a different content of the same revision, signed for that content. A change of what the guild gets (a character of mine joining the guild, the setting turned on or off, when it changes what that guild's members get) makes a new revision; when everyone else's record is unchanged, it is announced on that guild only, and the other audiences get the new revision at my next announcement to them (one `REC` with the same content as before). A character leaving the guild makes none: its members forget it once their roster drops it. The comparisons use the record's signed bytes without the revision (`Identity.RecordBytesWithoutRevision`); the new revision comes from `Identity.GuildContentChanged`, called when settings are held (`GuildSettings`) and when my character's guild is set (`Scopes`). Each content's signature is kept apart (`signatures`, see Saved Variables).
  - **Sending**: a `REC` on `GUILD` reaches members only, who all get the guild's content. On `PARTY` or `RAID`, requesters of both kinds get a `REC` each (fewest messages per content). The `GET`s ignored for 30 seconds after a `REC` (see Sending a Record) are only those of the requesters it reached with the content they get (each delivery keeps its guild). Who gets my record at all is `Scopes.RecordReaches`.
  - **Characters added by the rule**: in that guild, they act as linked for the guild only: their login, level-up and logout announcements go on `GUILD` (they confirm them), and they answer the guild's `GET`s with the guild's content. Elsewhere they send nothing, not even `NOID` for a character I unlinked: the players outside the guild learn its removal from my next revision announced by a linked character. `Identity.AnnouncedRevision` gives them the revision, and a new one when they are renamed or change ruleset, as for a linked character (`Identity.Refresh`); the audiences then hold my guild only, and `Scopes.AnnouncementReaches` / `Scopes.AnnouncesToGroup` keep the answers, friends added, group joined and whispers to it.
  - **Received**: a character marked `forced` is stored like the others, and forgotten as soon as my roster no longer lists it (`People.UpdateGuildMembers`); or as soon as none of my characters is in that guild any more (`People.GuildLeft`); a person left without the player's characters is forgotten (see Guild Lists, Members leaving). The guild's members show the person under its guild main (`Record.ShownMain`, kept as the person's `main`). Once the roster drops it, under the record's main, or, when the record has none, the first of its other characters by name (`keepShownMain`). A newer revision that no longer holds it removes it, as any character a record drops. At the same revision, a `REC` from my guild (on `GUILD`, or a guild member's whisper: content chosen for me as its member) whose signature differs from the one I hold has another content: it is verified and replaces mine (`People.ReplacesHeld`), so the guild's content wins whatever the order of arrival; on `PARTY` or `RAID` (content possibly meant for another requester) only the revision counts, as today.
  - **Joining a forcing guild**: what I hold of a member's identity was sent to me outside the guild, without its forced characters, and its revision doesn't change when I join. So, on joining a guild that enforces linking, the records I hold of identities with a character in that guild count as outdated (`People.MarkOutdated`, the person's `outdated`) and are fetched again (`People.IsWanted`: the revision I hold, while outdated): from the owner at its next announcement, or from the members holding them through the guild lists, so an owner never online at the same time still reaches me. Until fetched again, an outdated record is not passed on through the guild lists, so it never replaces the guild's content of the other members. The same applies when the first settings I hold of my current guild enforce linking: I joined it before they reached me, or I came from 0.1.x. Turning the rule on in a guild whose settings I already hold marks nothing: the members whose records change make a new revision. When an officer enables Who's Who and the rule in the same change, every member fetches what it holds of the guild once.
  - **Guild lists**: a record is passed on in the guild when it consents, or when it carries guild markers (`Record.HasGuildMarkers`); my own while the guild enforces linking, whatever my Guild scope. Lists only pass on within the guild, so what they carry is always the guild's content.
  - **0.1.x** refuses a record holding a field it doesn't know: a 0.1.x member of a guild that enforces linking keeps the last record it could read of a member whose record carries the markers, until it updates. Nothing to remove later.
  - **Opt-in.** Built: a guild enforcing linking accepts only complete identities, every character I have in it. Until mine is, nothing goes to that guild and nothing is taken from it, whatever my Guild scope (`Scopes.SharesWithGuild`, `Scopes.EnforcedGuild`: I opted in, or all my characters in it are linked with my Guild scope on, `Identity.AllLinkedInGuild`).
    - **Dialog** (`IdentityDialogs.GuildRule`), when `Scopes.EnforcedLinkingChoiceNeeded` says so: once a session, asked again after leaving the guild, out of combat (after `PLAYER_REGEN_ENABLED` when in combat), at login, at a roster update or when the guild's settings arrive; a rule turned on during the session, in a guild whose settings I held at login, waits for the next login unless I joined it this session. Whatever was chosen for the characters before (Not this character, unlinked). One dialog: shown in place of the first-login prompt, whose question it answers (the character I play is registered, linked or not). Its choices: **Link** them (turning the Guild scope on when it is off); **Share without linking** (the opt-in); **Refuse**: sharing with that guild is disabled. With several characters in the guild and my main not among them, a dropdown selects the guild main (`Identity.SetGuildMain`, saved as `main` in that character's guild).
    - **Consent** covers all my characters in that guild, saved per guild (`enforcedLinkingOptIns`, `Identity.SetEnforcedLinkingOptIn`: true opts in, false refuses; opting in or out makes a new revision); dropped when none of my characters is left in the guild or the rule no longer applies. A refusal goes back to unanswered when a character joins the guild (`Scopes.GuildRosterChanged`), so the rule is asked again then. Once my identity is complete there (opted in, or all my characters in it linked with the Guild scope on), the rule is accepted, and only leaving the guild undoes it: before a change that would leave it incomplete (the Guild scope turned off, a character in that guild unlinked, Forget me), the acceptance is saved (`Identity.KeepGuildRulesAccepted`, true), the guild keeps getting my characters in it, and a notice says so (`Identity.OnGuildRuleKept`). The completeness test is `Identity.CompleteInGuild`, used by `Scopes` for the current guild and for the alts of people known elsewhere.
    - **Guild tab**: a line at the bottom says what my current guild gets from me (`Scopes.GuildRuleAnswer`): while it enforces linking, my answer (accepted, refused, not answered) with **Answer** or **Change** (`IdentityDialogs.ShowGuildRule`) until it is accepted; otherwise whether my identity reaches it, with **Link** when no character is linked and **Share** when the Guild scope is off. With it, the guild gets the record of its members as built (my characters in it only, with the Guild scope off).
    - **No identity yet**: an identity without linked characters is still one, with nothing to share outside the guild; opting in shares my characters in that guild under it.
    - **Joining**: the game may give the roster before the guild's club ID; until it does, the character's guild has no ID (`Identity.GuildIdPending`), nothing goes to the guild (`Scopes.SharesWithGuild`), and the join announcement waits (`Protocol.GuildRosterChanged`; the roster is asked for again every 10 seconds until it comes with the club ID). With it, the rule of a guild whose settings I hold applies from the first message: a guild I refused or haven't answered gets only the empty announcement. The settings of a guild I never held settings of come from its members' answers to that announcement; until they arrive, that guild counts as not enforcing linking.
    - **Going back** on a choice: only a refusal, or an unanswered rule, can be answered again (Guild tab, see above); an accepted rule stays accepted until I leave the guild.
  - **Not built yet**: a member of a guild enforcing linking whom another scope allows (my Guild scope off, a friend in the guild) is entitled to my linked characters and my characters in the guild, but may pass on in the guild only the latter. That needs two records from me, held apart; until then, such a member gets the guild's record only: less than allowed, never more.
- **Guild lists.** Built: see Guild Lists. Each member keeps its own list: for each identity with consent and a confirmed character in the guild, its ID and the highest revision the member holds; and, apart, each guild record (planned, `guildEntries`), its ID and revision. The list's revision is `<server time>.<hash>`: when it last gained a newer identity revision, and a short hash of its content. The announcement carries it.
  - Mine older (or the same time with a lower hash): I ask **one** member who announced a newer list for it, take the highest revision per identity, fetch the records I lack (one `GET` may name several), then give my list a new time, so members holding an older list ask me in turn.
  - Mine newer or equal: I do nothing; several members asking me at once get one answer on `GUILD` (fewest messages, as for `REC`s).
  - Lists are hints, not data: every record is still verified with its owner's signature, so lists are not signed. A revision nobody delivers is dropped after the usual retry.
- **Relayed records.** Built (see Guild Lists). From a guild member who is not the record's owner: a newer revision of an identity I hold is accepted from any member; an identity I don't hold yet only from a member of the trusted rank, stored with every character listed until I see one. A record sent by the owner's own character is handled as today.
- **Guild records** (guild identities and guild links). Built: holding and applying them (Signed, Received), passing them on (Sending), the officers' changes (Changing), and what a player's record leaves to decide (below). The guild master and the officers, from the guild panel's Who's Who tab, create a guild identity for a character in the guild, and link characters in the guild to it or to a member's shared identity. The guild shows who is whose alt without enforcing linking, and keeps it up to date with as little work from its officers as possible. Shared with that guild only.
  - **One record per guild and identity**: what the guild says about one person (see Records, Guild record). About a member who shares an identity, it has that identity's ID: the same person, and an ID is enough to ask for either record, the player's (`GET`) or the guild's (`GIGET`). About someone without an identity, the guild makes one: a random ID `G<16 hex>`. A change sends that one record again, never the guild's whole set.
  - **Characters**: only characters in the guild. A character listed in a player's own record is that player's and is not linked by the guild.
  - **Signed** by the member of the trusted rank who last changed it, checked by `GuildRecord.Accept`: the signing character confirmed under the signer's identity (`People.IsConfirmedCharacter`, shared with the guild settings), its rank index in my own roster at most `GuildList.TRUSTED_RANK_INDEX`. A record refused because its signer isn't confirmed yet is not kept: the guild's next list asks for it again. Revision = server time, the most recent wins, the greater signer ID at the same revision (`GuildRecord.IsNewer`), so every member keeps the same one. Two officers changing the same identity at once: the later signature wins, for that identity only.
  - **Sending**: when an officer changes them, one `GIREC` on `GUILD` per record changed, at its latest revision, after the gathering of my changes to the guild, with its settings (`GUILD_CHANGES_WAIT`, see Protocol) (`Protocol.SendGuildRecord`; to each online member while my messages on `GUILD` reach no one); otherwise through the guild lists (`guildEntries`, see Guild Lists), asked for with `GIGET` from the members holding the list and answered with `GIREC`. Never outside the guild. Only while I exchange guild lists (`guildListsActive`): Who's Who enabled for the guild, my Guild scope, a linked character.
  - **Received**: from a member of my guild, about that guild. Accepted, it is held in `guildRecords`, and all that guild's records I hold are applied again, the most recent first (`People.ApplyGuild`; also at login, `GuildRecord.ApplyHeld`), so a person that disappeared (forgotten, its characters taken by another record) comes back from its record, and a character two records link goes to the most recent one (`GuildRecord.IsNewerThan`), on every member's client whatever order they came in. Two records linking the same character happen when two officers, not online together, link it to different identities: the guild master's or an officer's client, as soon as it holds both, unlinks it from the older record for everyone (`GuildRecord.LinkedByNewer`), right after a record of the guild changed (`Protocol.SendGuildRecord`, a `GIREC` accepted). A character that left the guild is unlinked the same way, for everyone, by the guild master's or an officer's client (`GuildRecord.LinkedFormerMembers`, from the roster as the game gives it at that moment, `Scopes.GuildMemberGuids`): when the roster's members change (`Scopes.OnGuildMembersChanged`; the first roster after login counts, for a member who left while no officer was online), and after a record of the guild changed; a record is never left listing someone outside the guild, and the character comes back only by being linked again. The older identity keeps its other characters, its main becoming the first of them by name when the main left, and goes when none is left: a character is in one record only, and nothing stays held that nobody sees. Each record: its characters go into the person's `chars` with state `guild` and that guild in their `guild` field, as my added characters do; the ones that guild linked and the record no longer lists leave. The player's `signedRecord` is never changed, and a new revision of it keeps them (`People.Accept`). A guild identity is a person of its own, `G…`, marked with the guild that made it (`madeByGuild`), with the guild's nickname and main; its main is always one of its characters: when a player's record takes it, or the roster drops it, the first of its other characters by name (`keepShownMain`), so that its row stays in the Guild tab, listed by its main. A link to a shared identity I don't hold yet waits in `guildRecords` and applies when its record arrives. A guild link wins over a `NOID` from the character (`People.DeclaredNoIdentity`) and over my own links: a manual identity holding the character merges into that identity, an alt I added elsewhere moves (automatic changes, as for a player's record; the same merge as for a player's record). A character a player's record declares, or another of that guild's records links, is not linked. Forgotten once my roster no longer lists the character (`People.UpdateGuildMembers`), and with its guild's records when none of my characters is left in it (`People.GuildLeft`, `GuildRecord.ForgetGuild`); a guild identity left without characters goes. A record without characters removes the guild identity, my nickname, note and added alts with it; its revision stays held, so an older copy is stale.
  - **Changing**: from the character I play, while I exchange guild lists and my roster shows it at a rank index up to `GuildList.TRUSTED_RANK_INDEX` (`Protocol.CanChangeGuildRecords`). Each change builds the record I hold anew (fields a later version added kept), or a new one, signs it with my identity, holds and applies it, like the guild settings (`GuildRecord.Link`, `Unlink`, `SetMain`, `Rename`, `Remove`). A new guild identity's ID is `G` and the first 16 hex characters of SHA-512 over the character's GUID, my identity ID and the server time (`GuildRecord.NewGuildIdentityId`); its first character is its main. A guild identity's main can't be unlinked while it has other characters (`GuildRecord.CanUnlink`), as a manual identity's; its main is the guild's only (`People.CanSetMain` refuses it).
  - **A player's record takes the characters it lists**: on every member's client, at once, whatever the guild record says (`storeCharacter`, shared by players' records, confirmations and guild records: who declares a character, weakest first, is me, my guild, the player, and a message from the character takes it whatever declares it; a stronger claim takes the character from another person, an equal one updates it on its own person. A guild-linked character follows the player's record silently, not being my link; a guild identity left without characters goes).
    - When players' own records hold every character of a guild identity (`GuildRecord.ClaimedByPlayers`), nothing is left to decide: the guild master's or an officer's client removes the guild identity for everyone, on its own, after a record of the guild or a player's record changed (`tidyGuildRecords` in `Protocol`, with the characters two records link). Its other fields would go to the guild's record about the player once the guild record holds any (a role, a note): none yet.
    - When they hold only some of them, the Guild tab shows it to the guild master and officers, on that guild identity, and they choose: move its other characters to the player (`GuildRecord.MoveTo`: the guild's record about the player links them, and the guild identity is removed; only when one player holds them), or keep them apart (`GuildRecord.Unlink` of the characters the players hold). Either answer ends it, which is read from the records each time: nothing else is saved.
  - **Open**: a character known both from a guild record and from a record received outside the guild, close to the two-scope member of Enforced linking (Not built yet).

### Guild Settings

A guild's settings (`GuildSettings`: `enable`, and `enforceLinking`, which needs it; see Enforced linking) are one record per guild, signed with the identity key of its guild master or one of its officers (rank index 0 or 1: `GuildSettings.LOWEST_RANK_INDEX`), and changed only from the settings view of the guild panel's Who's Who tab, whose **Who's Who settings** button those ranks alone see (`Protocol.ChangeGuildSettings`).

```
v (GuildSettings.VERSION, 2), guild (club ID), settings = { enable, enforceLinking }, signer (identity ID),
signerGuid (the character it was signed from), rev (server time when signed, or one more than the record held when that
is not later), sig (see Records, Signed bytes)
```

- **Sent**: a change goes to the guild after the gathering of my changes to the guild, with its records (`GUILD_CHANGES_WAIT`, see Protocol), as one `GSET` on `GUILD` (to each online member while my messages on `GUILD` reach no one, see Workarounds). Nothing goes out with the Guild scope off, and the tab's checkbox needs a linked character and the Guild scope: the members check the signer through its confirmed character.
- **Asked for**: every announcement carries `guildSettingsRev`, the revision held for the sender's current guild. A member of my guild announcing a later one than mine gets one `GSGET` by whisper; another member announcing it gets none while that one waits (`REQUEST_DELAY_SECONDS`), unless the first wasn't trusted for a guild's first settings and this one is: an officer, or who invited me, is asked over any other member. The member asked answers with its record by whisper. Members holding an older one do nothing: the older side asks. An announcement that accepts no `GET` (its sender is leaving) asks for nothing, and a revision I refused is never asked for again (`refusedGuildSettings`), so a member holding a record nobody accepts is not asked at each of its announcements. `Protocol.ChangeGuildSettings` signs nothing from a character my roster shows below `LOWEST_RANK_INDEX`. At login, the answers to my announcement bring the revisions of every online member, so at most one `GSGET` and one `GSET` follow.
- **Received**: from any member of my current guild, about it, relayed or not, whatever I share with the guild (`Scopes.GuildMember`): settings hold no identity. Right after joining, the game may not give my guild's club ID yet, and sends no other roster update by itself (checked in game): a record then waits, verified, with the records held back (below), and the roster is asked for again 10 seconds later until it comes with the club ID (`Scopes.GuildRosterChanged`); that roster update tries it again, and it is taken only if it is about my guild, so a member of the guild I joined can't replace the settings I hold of another guild. The guild rule's dialog needs that club ID too, so it shows no later. A record not newer than mine (`GuildSettings.IsNewer`: a later revision, or the same one from a greater signer ID) is dropped before its signature is checked; the others go through the verification queue, then `GuildSettings.Accept` with the signing character's rank index in my own roster (`Scopes.GuildRankIndex`): refused above `LOWEST_RANK_INDEX`; held back when the signing character is not a confirmed character of the signing identity yet, or not in my roster yet; accepted otherwise. A guild's **first settings** (none held, or held unconfirmed; joining a guild makes those I held from before count as first again, `GuildSettings.Joined`) usually come before I can confirm their signer (just joined, never linked, or a character that takes no record from the guild): they are taken unconfirmed (`signerUnconfirmed`) when their signer is at such a rank in my roster, whoever sent them, or when a member trusted for them sent them, whatever the signer's rank now (`Scopes.TrustedForGuildSettings`: an officer or the guild master in my roster, or the member who invited me, from `GUILD_INVITE_REQUEST`, kept for the session; that its name matches the add-on sender's is *to verify in game*). The next confirmed record replaces them whatever its revision; meanwhile any other revision a member announces is asked for (`GuildSettings.IsWanted`). Between two unconfirmed records, the later revision wins. Once the settings are confirmed, a newer record waits for its signer to be confirmed, whoever sent it. The newest record held back is tried again at each change of `People` and each roster update (memory only, lost at a `/reload`).
- **Not built**: checking the signer's rank again when the roster changes later, so a demoted signer's record stays until a newer one.

### Guild Lists

Members of a guild pass on to each other the identities guildmates shared with their guild, so each member recognises guildmates it has never been online with (see Guild Sharing for the rules). Only while Who's Who is enabled for the guild (Guild Settings), with the Guild scope, from a linked character (`guildListsActive`); otherwise the announcement carries `0` and nothing below happens.

- **My list** (`GuildList.Entries`): each identity I hold whose record consents (`guild.consent`) and that has a character `confirmed` or `relayed` in that guild, in the ruleset I play (the stored `guild`; `Scopes.KeepGuildMembers` sets it on the characters of a record stored after their confirmation, from the roster), with the highest revision I hold; mine too, while my record consents and the character I play is linked in the guild. And, apart (`GuildList.GuildRecordEntries`), each of that guild's records I hold, removals included, with its revision. The two together hold at most `MAX_LIST_ENTRIES` (2 000).
- **Its revision** (`GuildList.Revision`, saved in `guildLists`; forgotten when I join the guild, and when I leave it with none of my characters left in it, `GuildList.ForgetGuild`, so my next list of it is a first one): `at.hash`, the hash being the first 8 hex characters of SHA-512 over the sorted `id:rev` pairs of the identities, then of the guild records (`GuildList.Hash`). A 0.3.0 member hashes the identities only: in a guild mixing versions, each side takes the other's list once a session (`takenListHashes`) and finds nothing new. `at` is the server time of the last change of content, seen when the revision is next asked for (at the latest by the next announcement); a member's first list has `at = 0`, so that it is older than everyone's, and is never offered: until it first changes, the announcement carries `0`, no list. The same hash is the same list, whatever the times.
- **Comparing** (`evaluateGuildLists`, after the gathering of the announcements that came together, `ANNOUNCEMENT_WAIT`): each member's last announced revision is kept (`listOffers`), even while my lists are off, since the settings enabling them may come right after. When the most recent revision announced is later than mine (`GuildList.IsLater`: a later time, or the same time with a greater hash), with another hash, and that hash was not taken this session (`takenListHashes`, mine included), I send one `GLGET` to one member who announced it, a trusted one if any, chosen at random.
- **Answering**: `GLGET`s gather (`REQUEST_WAIT`, see Protocol); one `GLIST` on `GUILD` answers several, a whisper answers one. A `GLIST` on `GUILD` is used by every member, asked or not, unless its hash was taken or is mine.
- **Taking a list** (`takeGuildList`, `GuildList.Missing`): the identities it has at a newer revision than mine. The ones I hold are asked for from every member who announced that list: one `GET` of up to 3 identities to each in turn, from a random one, so that several members' requests spread over the holders (`requestFromHolders`); a batch not answered within `REQUEST_DELAY_SECONDS` goes once to the next holder. The ones I don't hold are asked for from the trusted members who announced that list only; when none did, from the most recent list a trusted member announced (one more `GLGET`); when no trusted member is online, they wait. The guild records it has that I lack or hold older (`GuildList.MissingGuildRecords`) are asked for the same way from every member who announced that list, with `GIGET`, apart from the `GET`s (`guildRecordRequests`: an ID can be asked for as a player's record and as the guild's at once): their signer's rank is checked whoever passes them on.
- **Relayed `GET`s**: a `GET` naming an identity in my list, from a member of my guild, gathers its requesters (`REQUEST_WAIT`, see Protocol); one `REC` on `GUILD` answers several, a whisper answers one. My own identity in a `GET` is answered as before. A `GIGET` is answered the same way with the guild's records I hold (`GIREC`, `answerRelayed` serving both).
- **Received `GIREC`s**: from a member of my guild while my lists are on (checked before unpacking), about my current guild, newer than the one I hold (`GuildRecord.IsNewer`); verified, then `GuildRecord.Accept` with the signing character's rank index in my own roster (see Guild Sharing, Guild records).
- **Relayed `REC`s**: used only while my lists are on, from a member of my guild, when the record consents. A newer revision of an identity I hold, from any member; an identity I don't hold, from a trusted member only (`GuildList.TRUSTED_RANK_INDEX`, rank index 1 for now, read in my own roster), its new characters then `relayed` (`People.Accept(signedRecord, fromGuild)`); a later message from one of them confirms it as any other.
- **Members leaving**: a shared person who had a character in the guild, and holds no character the player told me of (`confirmed` or `listed`, or `relayed` while my roster lists it), is forgotten with my nickname and added alts: once my roster no longer lists its characters (`People.UpdateGuildMembers`), and when none of my characters is left in the guild (`People.GuildLeft`). A person with a confirmed character stays. Until a character is confirmed, a person passed on by the guild shows the Guild glyph (`Lists.NicknameSource`). The same pass (`forgetFormerMember`) removes the characters that guild's records linked once the roster no longer lists them, and those another guild's records linked once the roster lists them (a character is in one guild at a time: it left that guild, whose roster I may not see before its next record arrives). A guild identity is forgotten once it is left without characters, and one that guild made when I leave the guild.
- **Turning lists on**: when the guild master or an officer enables Who's Who for the guild, its list is renewed (`GuildList.Renew`: `at` = now) and its announcement follows the settings on `GUILD`, asking for none back. The members accept the settings, compare with that one announcement only, and the ones holding another list send it their `GLGET`s, answered by one `GLIST` on `GUILD`; nothing else goes out all at once, and what the officer lacks spreads at the members' next announcements.
- **Leaving or joining a guild** empties the announced revisions and the hashes taken.

### Own Messages

Recognised by content: an announcement or a `REC` carrying my identity ID is mine (the `GUILD` and group echo) and is ignored; a `GET` is answered only for my identity ID, and none is ever sent for it; whispers only go to other players.

## Trust

### Rank Gate

- Guild ranks are compared by **index** (0 = guild master); a threshold means "this rank and every rank above".
- The trusted rank, for identities passed on by members (see Guild Lists), will be set in the guild settings record (see Guild Sharing); until then, it is rank index 1 (the guild master and officers, `GuildList.TRUSTED_RANK_INDEX`). The settings themselves are signed by rank index 0 or 1 only (see Guild Settings). Nothing is written in the guild info text.
- **Receivers** look the sender, or the signing character, up in their own roster. A claim of rank is never trusted.

### Signatures

**Decided: shared identity records are signed.** The code is in `Crypto/`:

- Ed25519 (RFC 8032) over SHA-512, pure Lua 5.1 on doubles, TweetNaCl algorithms. Curve constants are computed at load and checked by the vectors.
- In game (build 1.60.1): public key 120 ms, sign 122 ms, verify 249 ms. Offline under PUC Lua 5.1 it is about 2.5× faster.
- `Ed25519.SetSlicing(bits)` makes scalar multiplication yield inside a coroutine. At 16 bits per slice, one verify takes 33 frames at most 21 ms each. `RecordVerification.Queue(signedRecord, format, onVerified)` always runs this way, for identity records (`Record`) and guild settings (`GuildSettings`), one record at a time in a background queue (a frame's `OnUpdate`, hidden when idle). Its result is cached for the session by canonical bytes and signature, not by identity and revision, so a forged copy of a revision cannot block the genuine one.
- Not constant-time; side channels are out of scope in a game client.
- Possible improvement: faster field arithmetic, about 1.5–2× expected; not needed while verification is spread over frames.
- Key generation: `math.randomseed` does not exist on Forever (add-ons cannot seed `math.random`); the seed is SHA-512 over several local entropy sources.

The public key is the identity ID, and records can be relayed by anyone. A signature never proves character ownership; confirmations above still apply. The private key lives in SavedVariables, readable by any add-on; losing it means a new identity.

**Migrations** (`Migrations`), at load only, from `Store.Init`: numbered steps, one per older version, run once each and in order, for the data (`Migrations.Data`) and for the settings (`Migrations.Settings`); the number of the last step run is saved as `migrationVersion` in each (a later release's number is kept, so its steps don't run again after going back to it). A step only reads and changes the saved tables: what needs the game, the roster or sending is not a migration. Data: 0.1.0-beta.1's removal revision on an unlinked character (`removedInRevision`) becomes `removedAt`, and each `forgotten` revision `{ rev, at }`, both set to the migration's time (the real one is unknown); the club ID 0.1.0 saved as my character's `guild` becomes `{ id }`, without a join time; 0.1.1's single signature is dropped and its guild settings record moves into its entry; my identity, signed by 0.2.0 over a fixed list of fields, gets a new revision (see Records, Version 1); a character state `guild` saved by 0.3.0 (a player's record passed on by the guild) becomes `relayed`, `guild` now meaning what the guild itself links. Settings: 0.1.0-beta.1's `chatNicknames`, `tooltipNickname` and `tooltipOtherCharacters` (a boolean then, a table in development builds) move to these keys, and `debugMessages`, saved up to 0.1.1, to `diagnostic.enable`; the old keys are removed. In the settings, "Hidden" in the nickname and other characters dropdowns turns `enable` off and keeps the position or colour for when it is shown again.

Planned: `scopes.selected` and the list of selected players.

`linked = nil` means the character is not registered in the identity yet, linked or not. `Identity.Unlink` on such a character registers it unlinked (`linked = false`, no new revision): **Not this character** answers this way.

SavedVariables are written only on `/reload`, logout or quit. Nothing may depend on a save mid-session.

On `PLAYER_LOGOUT`, AceDB removes the values equal to their defaults, an empty table included, before the add-on's own handler runs. `people` and `whispers`, read at logout, are therefore not defaults: `Store.Init` creates them when missing. A setting read at logout falls back to its default (`Scopes.Get`, the whisper window).

## Offline Test Suites

WoW's sandboxed Lua has no test framework, so logic tests are plain-Lua scripts. They are dev-only: no TOC lists them and `.pkgmeta` strips `Tests/`. Each prints its `ALL ... PASSED` line and exits 0. Run from the add-on root:

```
lua Tests/Crypto-Test.lua
lua Tests/Model-Test.lua
lua Tests/Comm-Test.lua
lua Tests/Locale-Test.lua
```

`.github/workflows/checks.yml` runs luacheck and every `Tests/*-Test.lua` on each push.

Suites `loadfile` the shipping files with a fake `ns`. `Comm-Test` runs each logged-in character in its own environment (`setfenv`) over a simulated world (guild, friends, group, add-on channel, clock); LibSerialize and LibDeflate are replaced by stand-ins, since `Libs/` is not in the repository. `Tests/Support/Bit.lua` provides `bit` under plain Lua 5.1.

## Localization

Every player-facing string goes through `L[...]` (AceLocale). `enUS` is the default and fallback; other locales are in-repo files, `Locales/<locale>.lua`. Keys are the English text. Saved data and protocol fields are never localized.

## Workarounds

Code that works around a bug of the game, to remove once the game is fixed.

- **A new guild member sends nothing on `GUILD`** (WoW Forever, checked in game 2026-10-10). After joining a guild, a character's add-on messages on `GUILD` reach no one, not even its own echo, until it logs in again, though `SendAddonMessage` returns Success; it still receives the guild's, and its guild chat works.
  - What Who's Who does: from the join (`Scopes.GuildJoined`, in `Protocol.GuildChanged`) until the character's next login, `Scopes.CanSendOnGuild` is false: the join is saved with the character's guild (`guild.joinedAt`, `Identity.GuildJoined`), and `Identity.JoinedGuildSinceLogin` compares it with the character's `loggedInAt`, which a `/reload` keeps (a `/reload` does not end the bug) and `Identity.LoggedIn` sets at a login. The login announcement goes out at `PLAYER_ENTERING_WORLD`, once `Identity.LoggedIn` has run, rather than at `PLAYER_LOGIN`, which can't tell a login from a `/reload`. The broadcasts go to each online member (`guildMembers` in the audience, from `Scopes.OnlineGuildMembers`: the member who invited me first, then by rank, the guild master first, then by name, so the members trusted for the guild's first settings get them first; members the group channel reaches are left out); answers and `REC`s never choose `GUILD` (`fewestMessages`), so each requester gets a whisper. The join announcement waits for the guild's roster (`Protocol.GuildRosterChanged`), which gives the online members.
  - Checking whether the game is fixed: join a guild, `/ww debug on`, then `/run C_ChatInfo.SendAddonMessage("WhosWho", "test", "GUILD")`; it is fixed when the echo `Received, GUILD <name>: test` comes back and the other members receive it.
  - Removing it: `Scopes.GuildJoined`, `Scopes.CanSendOnGuild`, `Scopes.OnlineGuildMembers` and the audience's `guildMembers`, with their callers; `Identity.GuildJoined` and `Identity.JoinedGuildSinceLogin` (`guild.joinedAt` may stay; the login announcement may stay at `PLAYER_ENTERING_WORLD`); `Protocol.GuildRosterChanged` and its pending flag, the join announcement going back to one message on `GUILD` sent right away (`announceToChannel("GUILD")`); in Comm-Test, the `joinedGuild` flag and the cases that use it; this entry, and the Channels and Announcements sentences pointing here.
- **An add-on's entry in the guild roster's right-click menu taints the roster** (WoW Forever, found in game 2026-10-17). A menu turns off scrolling on the scroll list under the mouse when it opens, and back on when it closes (`Blizzard_Menu/Menu.lua`, `DisableScrollableRegions`, `RemoveMenu`), which lays that list out again. Picking an entry an add-on added (`Menu.ModifyMenu`) closes the menu from code that read the add-on's description (`Pick`), so for the roster's menu the roster's list is laid out tainted: its data and visible lines, then the member panel filled from them. Removing a member or editing a note from the roster is then blocked (`ADDON_ACTION_FORBIDDEN` on `RemoveFromGuild`, `SetNote`) until a `/reload`.
  - What Who's Who does: no Who's Who section in that menu (`MENU_UNIT_COMMUNITIES_GUILD_MEMBER` left out of `MENUS` in `UnitMenu`); the Who's Who tab of the guild window has its own menu.
  - Checking whether the game is fixed: put `MENU_UNIT_COMMUNITIES_GUILD_MEMBER` back, `/reload`, right-click a member in the roster and pick a Who's Who entry, then scroll the roster: `/run local s=CommunitiesFrame.MemberList.ScrollBox print(issecurevariable(s:GetView(),"dataProvider"))` stays `true` once fixed.
  - Removing it: put `MENU_UNIT_COMMUNITIES_GUILD_MEMBER` back in `MENUS`.

## Open Questions


- **Purging old people**: identities received through groups and whispers pile up in `people` over time, and so do `forgotten` and `noIdentity`. Everything needed to date them is saved (`lastSeen`, `updatedAt`, `forgotten.at`, the `noIdentity` times). Remove the people not seen for a long time (no activity on any of their characters) who are neither in my guild nor among my friends. To decide: how (a button in the settings, or automatic), whether people I created or renamed are kept, and whether the age is fixed or chosen.

- **False claims**: a modified client can list in its record a character that isn't the player's, for example a friend or guild member who doesn't use the add-on, to show it under its own identity. Covered: the character stays listed (unconfirmed), and a message from that character confirming another identity moves it there. A player who uses the add-on is safe, since their own identity is already known. Not handled yet: what to do once a false claim is detected; it matters if the add-on becomes popular.

## Common Pitfalls

- `/reload` does not pick up new files or TOC changes: restart the client.
- Never compare, concatenate or index with a value before checking `issecretvalue`.
- Registering an unknown event throws on Forever.
- Never trust a sender's statement about its own rank or about other characters.
- Bump `rev` on every change to a record, or peers will never fetch it.
